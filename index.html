Agentic CFD

Reports written by an AI agent that solved each problem with OpenFOAM, given only the problem statement. Technion final project, supervised by Prof. Steven Frankel.

1. Plane Poiseuille flowPASS
Steady, linear, exact solution. Velocity profile within 0.0061 % of the exact solution, 2nd-order convergence.
2. Lid-driven cavity, Re = 100WARN
Steady, nonlinear, benchmark (Ghia et al. 1982). Velocity profiles within 0.009 U of the benchmark; the remaining gap is traced to the benchmark's own mesh.
3. Stokes' first problemPASS
Transient, linear, exact solution. Velocity within 0.002 % of U, 2nd-order convergence.
4. Cylinder in a channel, Re = 100 (DFG 2D-2)WARN
Transient, nonlinear, vortex shedding, benchmark (FeatFlow). Drag, lift and Strouhal number all inside their uncertainty bands (St 0.3015 vs 0.3019); MPI scaling study.
4b. gpucfd next to OpenFOAM (same case as 4)WARN
The project's GPU solver on grids of equal and of clustered cells against OpenFOAM's body-fitted grid study (accuracy, cost, limits). gpucfd met the target set before the runs (drag 0.09 to 0.16 % below the published reference; three-grid study in 1,005 s with the implicit time scheme on the finest grid, 1,421 s without it, against about 1,743 s for OpenFOAM's study), but the reference is itself about 0.2 % low: against the value it is heading for, gpucfd is 0.31 to 0.38 % low and OpenFOAM within 0.04 %. OpenFOAM reaches the same drag accuracy in 153 s.
5. Cylinder in a square duct, Re = 20 (DFG 3D-1Z)FAIL
Steady, 3D, up to 2.4 million cells, benchmark (FeatFlow). Drag within 0.08 % of the reference; lift 1.7 % off, outside its band. A second mesh family shows the lift depends on the cell grading around the cylinder.
5b. GPU vs CPU study (same case as 5)PASS
OpenFOAM pressure solve on an RTX 4070 (OGL plugin) vs the best CPU setup. Same answer on both sides; the GPU breaks even at about 2.0 million cells and is 1.13 times as fast as the best CPU setup per iteration at 4.75 million cells. No pressure solver could gain more than 1.57 times there: the pressure solve is 36 % of an iteration.
6. Heated pipe, Re = 200, uniform wall heat fluxWARN
Steady, first heat transfer, exact solution (thermal entrance series). Local Nusselt number within 0.005 % RMS of the exact solution; fully developed Nu 0.011 % off, just outside the agent's own 0.01 % rule; gravity tested with a 3D run.
7. Turbulent flat plate, Spalart-Allmaras model (NASA TMR 2DZP)WARN
Steady, first turbulence model (RANS), NASA's own grids, benchmark (NASA Turbulence Modeling Resource). Grid-converged skin friction within 0.028 % of NASA's incompressible reference; just outside the 0.024 % grid-uncertainty band.
17. Turbulent channel, friction Reynolds number 180, no turbulence model (DNS)FAIL
Turbulence computed directly in time on gpucfd, outputs are time averages with a statistical error, benchmark (Vreman & Kuerten 2014, two independent codes). On the finest grid (192 x 64 x 64) the bulk velocity is 0.34 % below the reference, but all five profile limits set before the runs are missed (mean velocity up to 0.31 friction velocities off) and the three grids give no order of accuracy.
17b. Which grid gets the profiles right (same case as 17)WARN
The optimize stage after case 17, by the dev agent, one grid direction refined at a time, then one run on the grid that should pass, judged by case 17's own limits. A grid 1.5 times finer along and across the flow (288 x 64 x 96) meets all 10 limits of case 17 (the first grid met 5): mean speed +0.17 % with a statistical error of 0.19 %, in 1.7 GPU hours. One grid, no finer one to confirm it.
