1 Problem

A square cavity full of fluid is driven by its sliding top wall (the lid). Find the steady flow inside the cavity and compare it with the published benchmark solution of Ghia, Ghia & Shin (1982).

What we want to find

  1. aHorizontal velocity \(u\) along the vertical centreline \(x = L/2\), compared with Ghia Table I
  2. bVertical velocity \(v\) along the horizontal centreline \(y = L/2\), compared with Ghia Table II
  3. cStrongest return flow: minimum of \(u\) on the vertical centreline, and where it occurs
  4. dCentre of the primary vortex \((x, y)\) and its strength (minimum stream function)

Setup

  • Square cavity of side \(L\), completely filled with an incompressible Newtonian fluid.
  • The top wall (the lid) slides to the right at constant speed \(U\); the other three walls are stationary. All walls are no-slip.
  • The cavity is very long in the third direction, so the flow is two-dimensional.
  • Positions are scaled by \(L\), velocities by \(U\) and the stream function by \(U L\), as in the benchmark.

3 Theory

The cavity flow has no exact solution: the convection term makes the equations nonlinear. The comparison therefore uses the benchmark of Ghia et al. Theory still fixes what the answer depends on (one number, \(Re\)), what must hold exactly (no net flow through any line across the cavity), and the expected shape of the flow. The algebraic steps marked below are checked by SymPy.

Governing equations: continuity and momentum for a steady, incompressible, Newtonian fluid
\[ \nabla\cdot\mathbf{u} = 0,\qquad (\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla)\,\mathbf{u} = -\frac{1}{\rho}\nabla p + \nu\,\nabla^2\mathbf{u} \]
Mass is conserved, and each fluid parcel's momentum changes only through pressure and viscous forces. The left side (convection) makes the equations nonlinear: no exact solution is known.
Scaling: measure lengths in L, velocities in U, pressure in ρU²
\[ (\mathbf{u}^*\cdot\nabla^*)\,\mathbf{u}^* = -\nabla^* p^* + \frac{1}{Re}\,\nabla^{*2}\mathbf{u}^*,\qquad Re = \frac{UL}{\nu} \]
One number, Re, decides the shape of the flow (checked by SymPy below). This is why the benchmark reports x/L, u/U and ψ/(UL): those are the same for every cavity at Re = 100.
Boundary conditions (no slip)
\[ \mathbf{u} = (U, 0)\ \text{on the lid } (y = L),\qquad \mathbf{u} = \mathbf{0}\ \text{on the other three walls} \]
At the two top corners the wall velocity jumps from U to 0. There the velocity gradient is infinite (a corner singularity), so local errors near those corners shrink slowly with refinement in every method, including the benchmark.
Stream function: the requested measure of the vortex
\[ u = \frac{\partial \psi}{\partial y},\quad v = -\frac{\partial \psi}{\partial x},\qquad \psi = 0 \text{ on all walls},\qquad \int_0^L u(x, y)\,dy = 0 \]
Lines of constant ψ are streamlines, and the difference of ψ between two points is the flow rate between them. The walls are one streamline (ψ = 0). The lid drags the fluid clockwise, so ψ < 0 inside; its minimum marks the vortex centre and |ψ_min| is the flow rate circulating around it. Because no fluid crosses the walls, the net flow through any vertical line is zero: the forward flow under the lid is exactly returned lower down. This is a check on the simulation.
What else theory predicts (without numbers)
\[ Re \to 0:\ \text{flow symmetric about } x = L/2;\qquad Re > 0:\ \text{vortex shifted towards the lid's downstream side } (x > L/2) \]
Slow (Stokes) flow is reversible, so its pattern is mirror-symmetric about the vertical centreline. Inertia carries momentum in the lid direction and breaks that symmetry. Viscous-flow theory (Moffatt 1964, J. Fluid Mech. 18) also predicts small counter-rotating eddies in the bottom corners, with ψ > 0; the benchmark's Table V lists two of them (not requested here).
  • dimensionless momentum equation contains only \(1/Re\), with \(Re = UL/\nu\)
  • \(u = \partial\psi/\partial y,\ v = -\partial\psi/\partial x\) satisfies \(\nabla\cdot\mathbf{u} = 0\)
  • \(\int_0^L u\,dy = \psi(x, L) - \psi(x, 0)\), which is 0 because \(\psi = 0\) on every wall

7 Comparison

Benchmark: the simulation is compared with a trusted, very fine numerical solution of the same equations (Ghia, Ghia & Shin (1982), J. Comput. Phys. 48, 387-411). Differences are in units of the lid speed \(U\) and the cavity side \(L\); locations also in benchmark grid spacings \(h_G\).

How the differences are measured

Largest profile difference
\[ \Delta_{\max} = \max_k \left|\frac{u_{\mathrm{sim}}(y_k) - u_{\mathrm{Ghia}}(y_k)}{U}\right| \]
Worst case over the 15 (Table I) or 15 (Table II) interior benchmark points; the wall points are boundary conditions and are left out.
RMS profile difference
\[ \Delta_{\mathrm{rms}} = \sqrt{\frac{1}{K}\sum_{k=1}^{K}\left(\frac{u_{\mathrm{sim}}(y_k) - u_{\mathrm{Ghia}}(y_k)}{U}\right)^2} \]
Typical difference over the same points.
Observed order and Richardson extrapolation
\[ p = \frac{\ln\left|\frac{f_1 - f_2}{f_2 - f_3}\right|}{\ln 2},\qquad f_{\mathrm{ext}} = f_3 + \frac{f_3 - f_2}{2^p - 1} \]
From three meshes (1 coarse, 3 fine): how fast the answer settles, and the value it settles to (the zero-cell-size estimate). Profiles use one order from the RMS change between meshes.
Grid Convergence Index (GCI)
\[ \mathrm{GCI} = 1.25\,\frac{|f_3 - f_2|/|f_3|}{2^p - 1} \]
A conservative band for the mesh error of the finest result (Celik et al. 2008).

The remaining difference is not mesh error. Extrapolating to zero cell size changes the comparison little: the extrapolated profiles still differ from Ghia by up to \(0.005\,U\) (\(u\)) and \(0.0092\,U\) (\(v\)), while the uncertainty bands of the finest mesh are at most \(1.3 \times 10^{-4}\,U\). The gap is therefore not mesh error of this simulation. Its most likely source is the benchmark's own discretisation error (its grid, \(h_G = 1/128\), is coarser than the finest mesh here); the supplied data cannot prove this, and a second, independent benchmark would settle it. Until then the benchmark should be read as accurate to about this level, not to its printed digits.
Not a scheme artefact. The 128² case was re-run with central differencing for convection (Gauss linear) instead of linear-upwind. The two answers differ by at most \(1.9 \times 10^{-4}\,U\) at the benchmark points (and \(0.028\,\%\) in \(\psi_{\min}\)), while both differ from Ghia by up to \(0.0093\,U\). The scheme changes the answer far less than the gap to the benchmark, so that gap is not caused by the choice of convection scheme.
Where the differences are largest. In the thin layer under the lid, where \(u\) falls from \(U\) to the return flow over a few percent of the cavity (Figure 4, right panel), and near the downstream wall for \(v\). These are the regions of steepest gradients, next to the singular corners.
Physics check. The vortex centre lies \(0.12\,L\) right of the centreline and above mid-height: inertia carries the fluid towards the downstream wall before it turns down, as Theory predicted (a slow, inertia-free flow would be symmetric).
Two independent stream functions agree. \(\psi_{\min}\) from OpenFOAM's flux-based streamFunction and from integrating the cell velocities differ by \(8.7 \times 10^{-9}\) (relative) on the finest mesh.
Mass check, honestly. The check fixed before the runs ("\(|\psi|\) on the lid below \(1 \times 10^{-4}\)") failed with the velocity-integrated stream function (\(3.7 \times 10^{-4}\,UL\) on the finest mesh, concentrated in the last cell next to the top-right corner) but is exactly zero with OpenFOAM's flux-based one. It tested the post-processing at the singular corner, not mass conservation, so it was replaced by the net flow through the vertical centreline (see Insights).

productionlogged 2026-11-10 (time not recorded)
Problem
With residual targets \(10^{-9}\) (U) and \(10^{-8}\) (p) the 128 x 128 run stopped after 2288 iterations, but the probed velocities still changed by \(3\times10^{-7}\,U\) over the last 10 % of iterations, failing the physical steadiness criterion (\(10^{-7}\,U\)) fixed beforehand. The coarser meshes passed or were marginal.
Diagnosis
SIMPLE converges more slowly on finer meshes (iterations grow roughly like \(N^2\): 211, 660, 2288 for N = 32, 64, 128), so a fixed normalised-residual target leaves a larger change per iteration on fine meshes. A 10 % window on a slow, geometric approach to convergence then shows a larger change. Normalised residuals do not translate into a known solution error.
Fix
Kept the physical criterion unchanged (no moving the goalposts after seeing results) and tightened the residual targets by one decade (U \(10^{-10}\), p \(10^{-9}\)). That fixed 32²–128², but the 256² run (9481 iterations, about 10 min) then missed the criterion again (\(1.4\times10^{-7}\,U\) over its last 948 iterations), so the targets were tightened by one more decade (U \(10^{-11}\), p \(10^{-10}\)) and every mesh was re-run with identical settings. Two re-runs of the finest mesh cost about 20 minutes of wall time.
Lesson
Choose residual targets from the physical criterion, not the other way round; expect each refinement to need roughly one more decade of residual for the same solution change (the change per iteration falls more slowly on finer meshes). Check the criterion on the coarse meshes and extrapolate BEFORE starting the expensive finest run.
Guideline
SKILL.md stage 4: for steady runs, state that the stopping rule should be a physical quantity (probe change over a window) and that residual targets must be checked against it per mesh; ideally a function-object-based stop (e.g. runTimeControl on probe values) so the stop itself is physical.
toolinglogged 2026-11-10 (time not recorded)
Problem
Stopping an outdated background run (the Claude Code background task) killed only the Python wrapper; simpleFoam kept running as an orphan and would have kept writing into the case directory the restarted run uses.
Diagnosis
cfdlib.foam.run starts the solver with start_new_session=True (so a timeout can kill its whole process group). Killing the parent therefore does not reach the solver.
Fix
Found the orphan with ps, confirmed its working directory with readlink /proc/PID/cwd, and killed that one PID before restarting.
Lesson
Background OpenFOAM runs outlive their wrapper; check for orphans before restarting a case.
Guideline
cfdlib.foam.run: write the solver PID to <case>/solver.pid and install a SIGTERM handler that kills the process group; add a foam.stop(case_dir) helper and mention it in environment.md ("Long runs").

Problem

analysislogged 2026-11-10 (time not recorded)
Problem
The mass-conservation check I fixed beforehand ("max |ψ| on the lid below \(10^{-4}\,UL\)") failed with my own stream function: about \(7\times10^{-4}\,UL\) on the 128 mesh, halving with each refinement, although the solver's continuity errors are \(\sim10^{-8}\).
Diagnosis
My ψ integrates face-averaged cell-centre velocities up each vertical grid line. OpenFOAM's face fluxes differ from that average by the Rhie–Chow pressure term, which is large only next to the singular top-right corner (all of the discrepancy sits in the last cell under the lid at \(x \to L\); in the interior the two agree to \(\sim10^{-7}\)). So the check measured a post-processing reconstruction error, not a mass error. OpenFOAM's streamFunction utility, built from the conservative fluxes, is exactly 0 on all four walls (and the same sign and scale as Ghia's ψ, in m²/s).
Fix
Use OpenFOAM's flux-based ψ for output (d) and keep my reconstruction as an independent cross-check of \(\psi_{\min}\). The mass check is redefined (after seeing data, stated openly in the report) as the net flow through the vertical centreline, \(\int_0^L u\,dy\), from the same centreline profile used for output (a), which is far from the corners. The original lid-residual number is still reported.
Lesson
A conservation check must use the conservative quantities (face fluxes), or be evaluated away from singularities; otherwise it tests the post-processing.
Guideline
cfdlib.foam: add read_point_field and a structured-grid helper (cells and points by (i, j) index for a single-block blockMesh), and note in the primer that postProcess -func streamFunction gives a flux-based ψ in m²/s, zero on walls, same sign as Ghia.
