Key findings

  • Every requested quantity matches the exact solution on the finest run (2000 cells, \(\Delta t = 0.0025\,\mathrm{s}\)): velocity within \(0.00205\,\%\) of \(U\), wall shear stress, depth and momentum within \(0.00633\,\%\).
  • Halving the cell size and the time step together cuts the error by \(4\times\): observed order \(p = 2.00\) (design order 2).
  • The error is almost entirely spatial: on the finest mesh, refining the time step from \(0.02\) to \(0.0025\,\mathrm{s}\) changes the \(t = 1\,\mathrm{s}\) profile by at most \(0.00389\,\%\) of \(U\).
  • The wall shear stress cannot be matched at the very start (the exact value is infinite at \(t = 0\)). It is within 1 % of the exact value after \(0.015\,\mathrm{s}\) on the finest run and after \(1.04\,\mathrm{s}\) on the coarsest.
  • Momentum is conserved exactly: on every step of every run, the change of the water's momentum equals the plate's drag to \(7.8 \times 10^{-9}\) of the final drag.
LimitationThe plate is infinite and the start instantaneous, as stated. The infinite wall stress at \(t = 0\) cannot be reproduced by any finite mesh and time step; the first \(0.015\,\mathrm{s}\) of the stress history are less accurate than 1 %.
LimitationLaminar flow is assumed (the exact solution is laminar); whether a real layer at \(Re_\delta \approx 326\) stays undisturbed was not checked (no reference supplied).
LimitationThe very deep water is a \(50\,\mathrm{mm}\) layer with a shear-free top; valid while the motion has not reached it, i.e. well beyond the 20 s asked for.

1 Problem

A flat plate under a deep layer of still water is suddenly set in motion parallel to itself. Find how the motion spreads into the water over the first 20 seconds, and compare the simulation with the exact solution of this problem.

What we want to find

  1. aVelocity profiles \(u(y)\) at \(t = 1\), 5 and 20 s, compared with the exact solution
  2. bWall shear stress on the plate as a function of time for \(0 < t \le 20\) s, and its value at \(t = 20\) s
  3. cPenetration depth at \(t = 1\), 5 and 20 s: the distance from the plate where \(u\) has fallen to 1 % of \(U\)
  4. dTotal \(x\)-momentum given to the water per unit plate area by \(t = 20\) s

Setup

  • A very large flat plate lies at \(y = 0\) under a very deep layer of water, initially at rest.
  • At \(t = 0\) the plate starts moving in the \(x\) direction at constant speed \(U\), and keeps that speed.
  • The plate is so large and the water so deep that the flow depends only on the distance \(y\) from the plate and on time \(t\).
  • Far above the plate the water stays at rest.

3 Theory

This problem has an exact solution. SymPy reduces the equation to an ordinary differential equation with the similarity variable, solves it, and checks the result against the equation, the plate condition, the far field and the initial state. The requested quantities follow from it symbolically.

Governing equation: x-momentum for a flow that depends only on y and t
\[ \frac{\partial u}{\partial t} = \nu\,\frac{\partial^2 u}{\partial y^2},\qquad v = 0,\ \ \frac{\partial p}{\partial x} = 0 \]
With \(u = u(y, t)\) and \(v = 0\), continuity holds automatically and the convection terms \(u\,\partial u/\partial x + v\,\partial u/\partial y\) are zero: the Navier–Stokes equations reduce exactly to a diffusion equation. Momentum spreads from the plate into the water by viscosity, like heat from a suddenly heated wall.
Conditions: plate moves from t = 0, water initially and far away at rest
\[ u(0, t) = U\ (t > 0),\qquad u(y, 0) = 0,\qquad u(y \to \infty, t) = 0 \]
The plate jumps from rest to \(U\), so at \(t = 0\) the velocity is discontinuous at the plate.
Similarity: no length scale is given, so the profile depends only on \(y/\sqrt{\nu t}\)
\[ u = U f(\eta),\quad \eta = \frac{y}{2\sqrt{\nu t}}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad f'' + 2\eta f' = 0,\ \ f(0) = 1,\ f(\infty) = 0 \]
Profiles at different times collapse onto one curve when plotted against \(\eta\) (checked by SymPy below).
Solution
\[ u(y, t) = U\,\operatorname{erfc}\!\left(\frac{y}{2\sqrt{\nu t}}\right),\qquad \operatorname{erfc}\eta = \frac{2}{\sqrt{\pi}}\int_\eta^\infty e^{-s^2}\,ds \]
\(\operatorname{erfc}\) is the complementary error function: 1 at the plate, falling to 0 within a few units of \(\eta\).
Requested quantities
\[ \tau_w = \mu\left.\frac{\partial u}{\partial y}\right|_{0} = -\frac{\mu U}{\sqrt{\pi\nu t}},\quad \delta_{1\%} = 2\eta_1\sqrt{\nu t},\ \operatorname{erfc}\eta_1 = 0.01,\quad P = \rho\int_0^\infty u\,dy = 2\rho U\sqrt{\frac{\nu t}{\pi}} = \int_0^t |\tau_w|\,dt' \]
The wall shear stress is infinite at \(t = 0\) (the jump) and decays like \(1/\sqrt{t}\); its time integral is finite and equals the momentum in the water. \(\tau_w < 0\): the water pulls the plate back (\(\mu = \rho\nu\)).
Consequence for the numerics: the wall gradient
\[ \left.\frac{\partial^2 u}{\partial y^2}\right|_{0} = 0\ \Rightarrow\ \frac{U - u(h/2)}{h/2} = -\left.\frac{\partial u}{\partial y}\right|_0\left(1 - \frac{h^2}{48\,\nu t}\right) + O(h^4) \]
Because \(u\) stays equal to \(U\) at the plate, its curvature there is zero, so the two-point wall gradient a finite-volume solver uses (plate to first cell centre, distance \(h/2\)) is second-order accurate. Its own error is small once \(\sqrt{\nu t}\) spans many cells; at early times (\(\sqrt{\nu t} \sim h\)) it is not. The cell values themselves also carry an error of the same order, so this term is a scale, not the whole error.
  • with \(\eta = y/(2\sqrt{\nu t})\) the PDE becomes the ODE \(f'' + 2\eta f' = 0\) (no \(t\) left)
  • SymPy's solution with \(f(0) = 1,\ f(\infty) = 0\) is \(f = \operatorname{erfc}\eta\)
  • \(u = U\operatorname{erfc}\big(y/2\sqrt{\nu t}\big)\) satisfies \(\partial u/\partial t = \nu\,\partial^2 u/\partial y^2\)
  • \(u(0, t) = U\) (no slip on the moving plate)
  • \(u \to 0\) as \(y \to \infty\) (water at rest far away)
  • \(u \to 0\) as \(t \to 0^+\) for every \(y > 0\) (water initially at rest)
  • momentum balance \(dP/dt = -\tau_w\): the plate's drag is the only force on the water
  • \(\int_0^t |\tau_w|\,dt' = P(t)\) (impulse equals momentum)
  • \(\partial^2 u/\partial y^2 = 0\) at the plate
  • \(\eta_1\) from SymPy (nsolve) and SciPy (erfcinv) agree to \(10^{-12}\)
  • \(u_{yyy}/u_y = -1/(2\nu t)\) at the plate (gives the \(h^2/(48\nu t)\) term)

D3
A water layer of depth \(H = 0.05\,\mathrm{m}\) above the plate; one cell in \(x\) with periodic (cyclic) ends, one cell in \(z\) with empty faces: a 1D model in \(y\)
Physics
Why this choice
The plate is very large, so nothing varies along \(x\) or \(z\): periodic ends in \(x\) model an infinite plate, the empty direction removes \(z\). \(x\) must stay a real (cyclic) direction, because OpenFOAM does not solve velocity components in empty directions. \(H\) is about three times the 1 % depth at 20 s: the exact solution has \(u(H)/U \approx 3\times10^{-15}\) there (Theory), so the finite depth cannot be seen.
Effect on the result
Replaces the very deep water by a finite layer; the error this causes is the size of \(u(H)/U\), negligible.
Alternatives
A shallower layer (fewer cells for the same resolution, but closer to the edge of the motion); a graded mesh (fine at the plate, coarse above) for fewer cells.

7 Comparison

Verification: every run is compared with the exact solution of the same equations, at its own cell centres and time steps.

How the differences are measured

Profile error
\[ \varepsilon_u(t) = \max_i \frac{|u_i - u_{\mathrm{exact}}(y_i, t)|}{U} \]
Largest difference over all cells, as a fraction of the plate speed.
Wall-stress error
\[ \varepsilon_\tau = \frac{\big||\tau_{w}| - |\tau_{w,\mathrm{exact}}|\big|}{|\tau_{w,\mathrm{exact}}|} \]
Relative error of the plate-averaged wall shear stress from OpenFOAM's wallShearStress function object.
Momentum and depth errors
\[ \varepsilon_P = \frac{|P - P_{\mathrm{exact}}|}{P_{\mathrm{exact}}},\qquad \varepsilon_\delta = \frac{|\delta_{1\%} - \delta_{1\%,\mathrm{exact}}|}{\delta_{1\%,\mathrm{exact}}} \]
Relative errors; the depth is read from the profile by linear interpolation between cell centres.
Observed order
\[ p = \frac{\ln(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{coarse}}/\varepsilon_{\mathrm{fine}})}{\ln 2} \]
\(p = 2\) means halving cell size and time step cuts the error by 4.
Momentum balance
\[ \frac{\big|\,\mathrm{d}_t\!\int u\,dy - \tau_w/\rho\,\big|}{|\tau_w(t_{\mathrm{end}})|/\rho} \]
Per time step, with the solver's own time derivative (Euler on the first step, BDF2 afterwards). Zero up to round-off if every step is solved completely and no momentum is lost.

How the wall-stress error scales. The solver evaluates the stress from the plate to the first cell centre. Theory (last step) gives the truncation error of that two-point difference alone as \(-h^2/(48\,\nu t)\) (it would make the stress too low). The measured error at 20 s is \(3\)–\(3.01\) times the size of that term on every level, so it follows the same \(h^2/(\nu t)\) law, about \(h^2/(16\,\nu t)\), but the simulated stress is higher than exact: the error is dominated by the error of the near-wall cell values, not by the gradient formula. Because it depends on how many cells span the diffusion length \(\sqrt{\nu t}\), it is large in the first steps and falls with time.
Space versus time. With the time step fixed at its finest value, halving the cells gives order \(2.00\). On the finest mesh, halving the time step changes the 1 s profile by \(0.00298\,\%\), \(7.29 \times 10^{-4}\,\%\), \(1.8 \times 10^{-4}\,\%\) of \(U\) (order \(2.01\)); the time error left in the finest run is about \(5.93 \times 10^{-5}\,\%\) of \(U\), against a total error of \(0.00205\,\%\) at 1 s.
Momentum. The simulated momentum and the time integral of the simulated drag are the same number to round-off (Figure 5), so the momentum error \(1.95 \times 10^{-4}\,\%\) is the accumulated wall-stress error, not a conservation error.
