Summary ⚠ Every limit met, on one grid only

Question. Case 17 got the mean speed of a turbulent channel flow right but missed its profiles. What is the cheapest grid on this PC that gets the profiles right too?

A grid with cells \(1.5\times\) finer along the flow and across it (\(288\times64\times96\)) meets all 10 limits of case 17. The first grid (\(192\times64\times64\)) met 5 of them: its mean speed was right, its profiles were not. The run on the new grid took \(1.7\) hours on the desktop GPU. It is one grid, with no finer one to confirm it.

Why this verdict
  • the new grid is one grid, with no finer grid to confirm it: the last refinement along the flow still moved the mean speed by 0.8 per cent and the last across the flow by 0.6 per cent, in opposite directions
  • the time-step check of case 17 was not repeated on the new grid

Key findings

  • The cells across the flow were the main problem. Making only them finer brought 4 of the 5 profiles inside their limits (Figure 3).

1 What is compared

The flow is water-like fluid pushed between two flat plates, fast enough to be turbulent: full of swirls that keep changing. The solver (gpucfd, the project's GPU program) computes every swirl directly. Two research groups published very exact results for this flow (Vreman and Kuerten 2014); that is the reference. All runs here are the same problem with the same settings. Only the grid differs.

Words used on this page

  • Grid and cells. the box of fluid is cut into small cells, and the solver keeps one value per cell. It cannot see a swirl that is smaller than a cell. A grid is written as cells along the flow, toward the wall and across the flow.
  • Mean speed. how fast the fluid moves through the channel on average. One number.
  • Profile. a curve from the wall to the middle of the channel: how the velocity, or the strength of the swirls, changes with the distance from the wall. Harder to get right than the mean speed.
  • Swirl strength. how strongly the velocity jumps around its average at one height (the root mean square of the fluctuation). There is one for each direction.
  • Friction velocity. a speed built from the friction on the wall. All speeds on this page are multiples of it; the mean speed is about 15.7 of them.
  • Wall unit. the natural small length of the flow at the wall. Half the channel height is 180 wall units. The streaks of fast and slow fluid at the wall lie about 100 wall units apart.
  • Gap. the largest difference between our profile and the reference, anywhere along the profile.
  • Limit. how large a gap may be to pass. The solver agent of case 17 wrote the limits down before its runs; they were not changed here.
  • Time unit. half the channel height divided by the friction velocity: roughly the time a large swirl needs to turn over once.
  • Statistical error. a turbulent flow never repeats, so an average over a finite time is a little uncertain. This number says by how much.

Rules for a fair comparison

  • Same problem, same solver settings and same pass limits in every run. Only the grid differs.
  • The limits and the reference are those of case 17, fixed before that case's runs.
  • The target, the budget and the rule for picking the new grid were written down before the first run of this study.
  • Not like for like: the first grid's result is an average over \(38\) time units, the new grid's over \(30\), the one-direction runs' over \(10\).

2 Method

First find out which direction of the grid is too coarse, with three short runs. Then make one full run on the cheapest grid that should pass, and judge it by the limits of case 17.

M1
Refine one direction at a time
Why this choice
Three short runs, each with the cells \(1.5\times\) finer in one direction only, show which direction matters. Refining everything at once would not say where the cells are needed.
Alternatives
Refine all three directions together: projected at about 4 GPU hours for one run, with nothing learned about where the cost can be saved.
M2
Short runs of \(10\) time units for the three tests
Why this choice
The gaps of the first grid are much larger than the scatter of a short average, measured beforehand on the first grid's own record (for the mean velocity \(0.019\) against a gap of \(0.31\)).
Alternatives
Long runs for each test: three times the cost for the same ranking.
M3
Each run starts from the nearest finished flow field
Why this choice
The field of the first grid is interpolated onto the new grid, so the flow is already turbulent. After 4 time units its speed is scaled once to where the wall friction says it is heading, and it settles for 3 more. This start-up takes 7 time units; the runs of case 17 needed about 20.
Alternatives
Start from rest with a disturbance, or wait for the mean speed to drift into place by itself.
M4
Pick the new grid as the first grid plus the measured changes
Why this choice
The cheapest grid whose predicted gaps all stay within 0.8 of their limits. For the picked grid the prediction is just the first grid's result plus the change measured for each of its two refinements.
Alternatives
A formula that assumes the error falls with the square of the cell size. It failed its own check here: these grids are too coarse for that rule (section 5).
M5
The final run stops by a rule fixed beforehand
Why this choice
It ends when the statistical error of the mean speed is below \(0.25\,\%\) after at least \(30\) time units, or after \(2\) GPU hours.
Alternatives
A fixed run length chosen by eye.

With only the cells across the flow made finer, the swirl strength along the flow changes from too strong right at the wall to slightly too weak further out. It needs both refinements to come inside its limit.
The mean speed barely shows any of this. It is the profiles that tell a resolved flow from an unresolved one.

5 How sure is this result?

Meeting the limits on one grid is not the same as knowing the answer has stopped changing. Three checks say how far this result can be trusted.

Result
No. Each last step still moved the mean speed by more than half a per cent, in opposite directions.
What it means
About half a per cent of grid error is probably left from each direction, and the two partly cancel. The mean speed of the new grid is trustworthy to about half a per cent, not to the \(0.17\,\%\) it happens to show. A finer grid would settle it.

The estimate assumes the error falls with the square of the cell size, which these grids do not fully obey. The run with finer cells along the flow only had not fully settled, so the second row is less certain.

7 Insights

Performance and GPU entries of the case's live insight log: what limited speed, what helped, what to try next. Each entry shows its problem; open it for the diagnosis, the fix and the lesson.

gpu logged 2026-11-14 13:25
A gap given as one number could not be split into shares of the three directions
Diagnosis
The gap is the largest difference along a profile. Its place moves from run to run and its sign with it, so the changes of that one number do not add up.
Fix
The comparison is made at each height of the profile, and the largest difference is taken at the end.
Lesson
Compare profiles point by point when refining one direction at a time.
Guideline
dev/LOOP.md, step 8.
gpu logged 2026-11-14 13:30
The planned formula for predicting a grid failed its own check
Diagnosis
It assumes the error falls with the square of the cell size. Carried to infinitely fine cells it left a mean-velocity gap several times the scatter, so these grids are too coarse for that rule.
Fix
The formula was used only to rank the directions. The grid was picked as the first grid plus the two measured changes.
Lesson
Check a prediction model on its own data before trusting what it predicts.
Guideline
gpucfd/DESIGN.md, channel section.
gpu logged 2026-11-14 13:35
One run was still speeding up while it was averaged
Diagnosis
The run with finer cells along the flow was scaled to its expected speed after 4 time units, while its field was still adapting to the new grid. The scaling came too early for that run.
Fix
The final run started from that run's field, the nearest one, and its wall friction then matched the driving force.
Lesson
When the wall friction is still off after settling, scale once more.
Guideline
gpucfd.turbulence.rescale_to_balance (its text).
