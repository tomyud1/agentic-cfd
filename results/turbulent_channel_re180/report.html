1 Problem

A fluid flows between two parallel smooth plates, driven by a constant force along the plates. The flow is turbulent. No turbulence model is used: the eddies are to be computed directly in time, and the answers are averages over time. Find the mean velocity profile, the mean speed through the channel, the friction coefficient and the strength of the velocity fluctuations, each with its statistical uncertainty, and compare them with a published simulation of the same flow in the same box.

What we want to find

  1. aBulk velocity \(U_b/u_\tau\) and bulk Reynolds number \(Re_b\), with the statistical uncertainty of the time average
  2. bFriction coefficient \(C_f\)
  3. cMean velocity on the centre plane, \(U_c/u_\tau\)
  4. dMean velocity profile in wall units, \(u^+\) against \(y^+\), with the law of the wall
  5. eRoot-mean-square velocity fluctuations \(u'_{rms}\), \(v'_{rms}\), \(w'_{rms}\) in wall units against \(y^+\); the peak of \(u'_{rms}\) and where it is
  6. fTurbulent shear stress \(-\langle u'v'\rangle/u_\tau^2\) and the total shear stress across the channel, against the exact straight line
  7. gMean wall shear stress against the driving force: how well the time average has settled
  8. hIs the turbulence resolved? State the difference that remains to the reference and what it is made of (grid, averaging time, the reference's own uncertainty)

Setup

  • Three-dimensional. Coordinates: \(x\) along the flow, \(y\) normal to the plates, \(z\) across the flow. The plates are at \(y = 0\) and \(y = 2\delta\); \(\delta\) is half the distance between them.
  • Domain: the box \(L_x \times 2\delta \times L_z\) with \(L_x = 4\pi\delta\) and \(L_z = \tfrac{4}{3}\pi\delta\). The flow repeats in \(x\) and in \(z\) (periodic), which stands for a channel that is very long and very wide. The reference uses the same box.
  • Plates: smooth, at rest, no slip.
  • Driving: a uniform, constant force per unit mass \(f\) along \(x\) (the same as a constant mean pressure gradient \(-\mathrm{d}P/\mathrm{d}x = \rho f\)). The flow rate is not prescribed: it is a result.
  • In a statistically steady state the two plates carry the driving force, so the mean wall shear stress is known in advance: \(\tau_w = \rho f \delta\). The friction velocity is therefore \(u_\tau = \sqrt{\tau_w/\rho} = \sqrt{f\delta}\).
  • Friction Reynolds number: \(Re_\tau = u_\tau \delta / \nu = 180\).
  • Incompressible fluid of constant density and viscosity.
  • The flow must be turbulent. The same equations also have a laminar solution (a parabolic profile with the centre speed \(\tfrac12 Re_\tau u_\tau = 90\,u_\tau\)); it is not the answer wanted. The initial state is free to choose.
  • No turbulence model and no wall model: all eddies are resolved by the grid and the time step (direct numerical simulation).
  • Averages \(\langle\cdot\rangle\): over \(x\), \(z\), time, and the two halves of the channel, taken after the start-up transient. Fluctuations: \(u' = u - \langle u\rangle\), likewise for \(v\) (along \(y\)) and \(w\) (along \(z\)).
  • Wall units: \(u^+ = u/u_\tau\), \(y^+ = y\,u_\tau/\nu\) with \(y\) the distance from the nearer plate.
  • Bulk velocity: \(U_b = \frac{1}{2\delta}\int_0^{2\delta} \langle u\rangle\,\mathrm{d}y\). Friction coefficient: \(C_f = \tau_w / (\tfrac12 \rho U_b^2)\). Bulk Reynolds number: \(Re_b = 2\delta\,U_b/\nu\).
  • Total shear stress: \(\tau(y) = \rho\nu\,\mathrm{d}\langle u\rangle/\mathrm{d}y - \rho\langle u'v'\rangle\). In a statistically steady state the mean momentum balance gives exactly \(\tau(y) = \tau_w\,(1 - y/\delta)\).

3 Theory

A turbulent flow has no exact solution: it is chaotic, and only its averages are reproducible. Theory still fixes several things exactly, and they are what the simulation is checked against apart from the reference: the wall shear stress is set by the driving force, the total shear stress is a straight line, the profile at the wall is known, and the balance of the whole box says how slowly the bulk velocity settles and how large the statistical error of its average is. SymPy derives and checks each of them.

Governing equations: incompressible flow driven by a constant force
\[ \nabla\cdot\mathbf{u} = 0,\qquad \frac{\partial \mathbf{u}}{\partial t} + (\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla)\,\mathbf{u} = -\frac{1}{\rho}\nabla p + \nu\,\nabla^2\mathbf{u} + f\,\mathbf{e}_x \]
Solved as they stand, in three dimensions and in time, with no model added. The turbulent flow is chaotic: no formula gives it, and two runs never agree instant by instant. Only its averages are reproducible, so every requested output is an average.
Average over x, z and time: the mean momentum balance
\[ 0 = f + \frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}y}\left(\nu\,\frac{\mathrm{d}\langle u\rangle}{\mathrm{d}y} - \langle u'v'\rangle\right)\quad\Rightarrow\quad \frac{\tau(y)}{\rho} = \nu\,\frac{\mathrm{d}\langle u\rangle}{\mathrm{d}y} - \langle u'v'\rangle = f\,(\delta - y) \]
Exact for a settled flow. The total shear stress (viscous part plus the part carried by the eddies) is a straight line from the wall to zero on the centre plane, and its wall value is \(\tau_w = \rho f\delta\). Theory does not say how the total splits into its two parts; that is the turbulence, and the simulation has to find it.
The laminar solution of the same equations (not the answer wanted)
\[ U_{lam}(y) = \frac{f}{2\nu}\,y\,(2\delta - y),\qquad U_{c,lam} = \tfrac12 Re_\tau\,u_\tau,\qquad U_{b,lam} = \tfrac13 Re_\tau\,u_\tau \]
Without eddies the whole stress is viscous and the profile is a parabola. The start field must push the flow away from this solution, which is stable to small disturbances.
Right at the wall the eddies die out and the profile is known
\[ u^+ = y^+ - \frac{(y^+)^2}{2\,Re_\tau} + O\!\left((y^+)^4\right),\qquad u^+ = \frac{\langle u\rangle}{u_\tau},\quad y^+ = \frac{y\,u_\tau}{\nu} \]
No slip and continuity make the turbulent stress vanish like \(y^3\) at the wall, so the viscous stress carries everything there. Further out (roughly \(y^+ > 30\)) experiments and simulations follow the logarithmic law \(u^+ = \tfrac{1}{\kappa}\ln y^+ + B\); the constants are empirical (\(\kappa \approx 0.41\), \(B \approx 5.2\), textbook values quoted from memory of Pope, Turbulent Flows, 2000, so approximate), and at this low Reynolds number the logarithmic region is short. The law is drawn for orientation, not used as a check.
The whole box: why the bulk velocity settles slowly
\[ \frac{\mathrm{d}U_b}{\mathrm{d}t} = f - \frac{\tau_{w,low} + \tau_{w,high}}{2\,\rho\,\delta}\quad\Rightarrow\quad T_s = \frac{\delta\,U_b}{n\,u_\tau^2}\ \text{ for } \tau_w \propto U_b^{\,n} \]
Exact at every instant (the periodic sides cancel). Averaged over a window it says: mean wall shear minus driving force equals minus the change of the bulk velocity over the window, divided by its length. So the averaged wall shear against the driving force measures how well the average has settled (request g). The exponent \(n\) comes from an empirical friction law and is used only to plan run lengths.
An average over a finite time has a statistical error
\[ \sigma_{\overline{q}} \approx \sigma_q\,\sqrt{\frac{2\,T_{corr}}{T_{avg}}} \]
Successive samples of a turbulent signal resemble each other for about its memory \(T_{corr}\), so an average over \(T_{avg}\) holds only about \(T_{avg}/(2T_{corr})\) independent samples. No grid refinement removes this error; only a longer average does, with the square root of the time. Every result below is quoted with it, estimated from the scatter of block averages (cfdlib.signals.stationary). That estimate needs blocks several memories long.
The bulk velocity has a long memory: its error from the balance of the box
\[ \frac{\mathrm{d}U_b}{\mathrm{d}t} = -\frac{U_b - U_{eq}}{T_s} + \xi(t)\quad\Rightarrow\quad \overline{U_b} - U_{eq} = T_s\left(\overline{\xi} - \frac{\Delta U_b}{T_{avg}}\right),\qquad \sigma_{\overline{U_b}} = T_s\,\sigma_{\overline{\xi}} \]
Near its settled value the bulk velocity is pulled back with the settling time \(T_s\) and pushed by \(\xi\), what the eddies add to the wall shear from moment to moment. So the memory of the bulk velocity is \(T_s\), about nine time units: a run of 40 time units holds about two independent samples of it, and blocks cannot be made long enough. The push \(\xi = f - \tau_w/(\rho\delta) + (U_b - \overline{U_b})/T_s\) is computed from the recorded wall shear and bulk velocity; it has a short memory, so its mean has a sound block estimate, and \(T_s\) times that is the error of the mean bulk velocity. This was added after two runs of the coarsest grid disagreed by five times the plain block estimate (Section 5).
  • total stress \(\tau/\rho = f(\delta - y)\) satisfies \(\mathrm{d}(\tau/\rho)/\mathrm{d}y = -f\) and vanishes on the centre plane
  • wall value \(\tau_w/\rho = f\delta\), so \(u_\tau = \sqrt{f\delta}\) and \(\tau = \tau_w(1 - y/\delta)\)
  • laminar profile \(U = f\,y(2\delta - y)/(2\nu)\) satisfies \(\nu U'' = -f\) and no slip on both plates
  • laminar centre speed \(= \tfrac12 Re_\tau u_\tau\) and laminar bulk speed \(= \tfrac13 Re_\tau u_\tau\)
  • near the wall \(u^+ = y^+ - (y^+)^2/(2Re_\tau)\) (up to terms of order \((y^+)^4\))
  • a small gap of the bulk velocity decays at the rate \(n u_\tau^2/(\delta U_b)\) when \(\tau_w \propto U_b^{\,n}\)
  • a relative gap \(g/U_b\) of the bulk velocity shows as a wall-shear imbalance of \(n\,g/U_b\)
  • window mean: \(\overline{U_b} - U_{eq} = T_s\,(\overline{\xi} - \Delta U_b/T)\), so its statistical error is \(T_s\) times that of \(\overline{\xi}\)
  • three-level study equals cfdlib.verification.three_grid_study for equal ratios
  • three-level study recovers a known order and limit for the ratios 1.5 and 4/3

Physical model
D1
Incompressible flow of a fluid with constant density and viscosity, solved in three dimensions and in time, with no turbulence model and no wall model (direct numerical simulation)
Physics
Why this choice
The problem asks for exactly this. At \(Re_\tau = 180\) the smallest eddies are only a few hundred times smaller than the channel, so a grid can carry all of them and nothing has to be modelled.
Effect on the result
Defines the equations. Every eddy that the grid cannot carry is an error of the grid, not of a model; the grid study measures it.
Alternatives
A turbulence model (RANS, as in case 7) gives the mean flow in minutes but models the quantities asked for here (the fluctuations). A large-eddy simulation models only the smallest eddies; gpucfd has no such model.
OpenFOAM settingsrun.py make(): gcfd.NavierStokes(nu=...)problem.yaml given.nu, given.rho
Solver
D2
gpucfd, the project's GPU flow solver (staggered grid, second-order central differences in the energy-conserving form, pressure by multigrid), instead of OpenFOAM
Solver
Why this choice
The problem fits what gpucfd covers (cfdlib.gpu.check_fit: fits, no blockers). The cost is steps times time per step, tens of thousands of steps per run: gpucfd was measured 2 to 9 times faster per step than OpenFOAM on this flow (gpucfd/DESIGN.md, OpenFOAM at its tutorial settings on 8 cores), and its scheme adds no numerical damping, which matters when the eddies themselves are the answer. OpenFOAM's tutorial set-up for this flow (two pressure corrections per step) was first order in time on case 4.
Effect on the result
How the answer is reached, and through the scheme also its accuracy per cell: central differences without damping keep the energy of the eddies but need every level of the grid study to carry them.
Alternatives
OpenFOAM pimpleFoam without a model: the same equations, several times slower per step here, and its accuracy on such grids was not measured.

D3
The box \(4\pi\delta \times 2\delta \times \tfrac{4}{3}\pi\delta\) of the problem and of the reference; periodic along \(x\) and \(z\); no slip on both plates; a constant force \(f\) per unit mass along \(x\)
Physics
Why this choice
As given. The periodic sides stand for a channel that is very long and wide; the reference uses the same box, so any effect of its size is the same on both sides of the comparison. A constant force (not a prescribed flow rate) fixes the wall shear stress, and the flow rate is a result.
Effect on the result
Defines the problem. Eddies longer than the box are cut off, on both sides of the comparison alike.
Alternatives
A prescribed flow rate with the force adjusted every step: settles faster, but it is a different problem (the friction velocity becomes a result) and gpucfd has no such control.

Grid
D4
One family of three grids, \(96 \times 32 \times 32\), \(144 \times 48 \times 48\), \(192 \times 64 \times 64\) cells (\(x \times y \times z\)): equal cells along \(x\) and \(z\), cells clustered toward both plates by a tanh law with a wall cell of \(2.00\), \(1.33\), \(1.00\) wall units
Accuracy
Why this choice
Turbulence at a wall lives in thin streaks about 100 wall units apart and needs cells of a few wall units in \(y\) at the wall, so the grid is judged in wall units (the table in the Simulation section). Three times as many cells along \(x\) as along \(z\) makes the cells square in the wall plane, which halves the work of the pressure solver (gpucfd/DESIGN.md). All three directions are refined together, with the same stretching, so the three levels are one family and their differences measure the grid error. The finest level is the one gpucfd's exploration found within about 1 % in the mean flow.
Effect on the result
The main numerical error of the result. The reference used cells two to four times smaller in every direction.
Alternatives
Three levels with ratio 2 (the workflow's default) would end at \(384 \times 128 \times 128\), 6.3 million cells and many hours; here the ratios are 1.5 and 4/3. Cells twice as long as wide (the usual choice for this flow) would resolve the streaks better per cell but double the pressure work; not tested.

Time integration
D5
Implicit diffusion (Crank-Nicolson) with explicit three-stage convection (scheme="imex"); the time step is re-chosen every \(5\) steps to hold a Courant number of \(0.75\)
Accuracy
Why this choice
With cells of 1 wall unit at the plates the explicit scheme's viscous limit would set the step; the implicit treatment removes that limit, so only the flow speed limits the step. That limit moves as the eddies come and go, so the step follows it. The first complete run (target 0.8, a look every 10 steps) passed a Courant number of 1 at a few moments, because the largest local value can rise by a third within 10 steps; hence a look every \(5\) steps and the target \(0.75\). The step is far below the time scales of the eddies; the time-step check (the coarsest grid again with half the step) measures what is left.
Effect on the result
The error in time, and the cost: the number of steps is the Courant number's inverse.
Alternatives
A fixed step sized for the worst moment wastes about a third of the steps; the explicit scheme needs a several times smaller step on these grids.
OpenFOAM settingsnumerics.yaml scheme, time.cfl, time.dt_cap, time.steps_per_sample, time.store_everyrun.py run_one(): gpu.run_transient(adapt_cfl=...)
Pressure solve
D6
Pressure tolerance \(10^{-6}\) (relative residual of each projection), mixed precision multigrid
Accuracy
Why this choice
Chosen by the same-answer check on the coarsest and the finest grid (cfdlib.gpu.tolerance_check, Pre-flight section): the same stretch of flow computed with five tolerances. With this one, bulk velocity and wall shear differ from the strictest run by \(1.7 \times 10^{-12}\) on the finest grid (rule: \(1 \times 10^{-4}\)), and the divergence left in the velocity is \(1.2 \times 10^{-10}\) of the check's scale. A step is \(9\,\%\) cheaper than with the tolerance first planned.
Effect on the result
How exactly the velocity is free of divergence after each step. The check covers a short stretch; over a long run two turbulent flows part ways whatever the tolerance, so only the divergence check of each run speaks for the long term.
Alternatives
A stricter tolerance: same answer over the stretch tested, up to a third more time per step.

Solver
Why this choice
The problem leaves the initial state free. Effect on the result
Nothing in a settled average, provided the start-up is cut. Where the cut is placed is a choice of its own (next decision).

Every grid from the same artificial start field: independent runs, but each pays the transition again.

Averaging and stopping
D8
Plane averages are sampled every \(5\) steps. The start-up is cut at the later of the point cfdlib.signals.settle_time finds and the time at which the bulk velocity's slow approach has decayed to \(0.2\,\%\) of its value. A run ends when, over the rest: the standard error of the mean bulk velocity, taken from the balance of the box, is below \(0.25\,\%\); at least \(36\) time units are averaged; the two halves of the record agree within \(2\) standard errors; and the mean wall shear is within \(0.3\,\%\) of the driving force
Accuracy
Why this choice
A turbulent flow never becomes steady, so a run cannot stop on residuals or on a value that stops changing. What can settle is the average and its error. The conditions are the error wanted, an averaging time of about four settling times, no remaining trend, and the flow's own balance (wall shear against driving force), which is the physical check. This is the second version of the rule. The first, fixed before the runs, took the error from block means of the bulk velocity and asked for 0.1 %; two complete runs of the coarsest grid then disagreed by five times that error, because the bulk velocity remembers for about nine time units (Theory). The error is now formed from a short-memory signal and the settling time, and the limit is what tens of time units can deliver.
Effect on the result
The statistical error of every output, and the run time: halving the error needs four times the averaging time (0.1 % would need about 300 time units).
Alternatives
A fixed run length chosen in advance (the usual practice): simpler, but it does not say whether the average has settled. Several independent shorter runs: the same statistics and a direct error estimate from their scatter, at the price of one start-up each.
OpenFOAM settingsnumerics.yaml stoprun.py start_up_end(), bulk_average(), stop_state(), make_watch()

7 Comparison

Benchmark: comparison with the direct numerical simulations of Vreman and Kuerten (2014) of the same flow in the same box, two independent codes on far finer grids, averaged over 200 time units. Every difference is judged against limits fixed before the runs and is shown with the statistical error of this simulation's average.

How the differences are measured

Difference
\[ \Delta q = \frac{q_{sim} - q_{ref}}{q_{ref}},\quad q_{ref} = \frac{q_{S} + q_{FD}}{2} \]
Relative difference of a number from the mean of the reference's two codes (S: spectral, FD: finite differences). For profiles: the largest absolute difference along the profile, in wall units.
Statistical error
\[ \sigma_{\overline{U_b}} = T_s\,\sigma_{\overline{\xi}},\quad \sigma_{\overline{q}} = \frac{\sigma_{blocks}}{\sqrt{N_{blocks}}} \]
One standard error of a time average. For the bulk velocity (and with it the mean velocity everywhere) from the balance of the box; for the other outputs from the scatter of block means.
Grid study
\[ q_k = q_0 + C\,h_k^{\,p}\ \ (k = 1, 2, 3)\quad\Rightarrow\quad p,\ q_0 \]
Three grids with cell sizes \(h_k\) give the observed order \(p\) and the value \(q_0\) extrapolated to zero cell size, when the three values approach a limit from one side.
Fluctuation
\[ u'_{rms} = \sqrt{\langle (u - \langle u\rangle)^2\rangle},\qquad \langle\cdot\rangle:\ x,\ z,\ t,\ \text{both halves} \]
About the full mean, as in the reference. The same for \(v\) and \(w\).

Problems met while producing this report, logged at the moment they happened, with how they were solved and what future agents should do differently. These feed back into the workflow guidelines.

setuplogged 2026-11-14 08:28
Problem
The machine file says a single run projected above about 15 minutes is a reason to coarsen the plan. A turbulent flow computed without a model cannot meet that. Its outputs are time averages, the bulk velocity needs tens of time units to settle and to average, and the finest grid that the exploration in gpucfd/DESIGN.md found usable (192 by 64 by 64 cells) costs about one minute per time unit.
Diagnosis
The 15 minute rule was written for runs that end when they converge. Here the length of a run is set by the physics (the bulk velocity forgets a disturbance in about 9 time units, theory.py) and by the statistical error wanted, not by the grid alone. Coarsening below 192 by 64 by 64 would give up the accuracy the problem asks about.
Fix
Kept the finest level at 192 by 64 by 64 and planned about one hour for it; kept the two coarser levels cheap (ratio 1.5 and 4/3 instead of 2). Said so in the decisions and in the limitations. Every run still ends on a stop rule fixed in advance, with a longest time per level.
Lesson
For averaged turbulence the budget is time units times cost per time unit. Plan it from the settling time and the statistical error, then choose the grid that fits.
Guideline
Add to the machine file, next to the 15 minute rule, a budget for runs whose outputs are time averages (for example a total per case), and say in WORKFLOW.md stage 3 that the pre-flight of such a run should project seconds per time unit and the time units needed, not only the time to a fixed end.
