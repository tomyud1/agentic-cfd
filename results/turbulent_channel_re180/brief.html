Turbulent channel flow without a turbulence model: OpenFOAM and gpucfd

Verdict of the case: FAIL

Goal

Simulate a turbulent flow directly, with every swirl computed and no turbulence model, for the first time in this project, and see how accurate and how fast each of our two tools is on it.

OpenFOAM computed the project's first turbulent flow earlier, with a turbulence model (case 7, a flat plate). For this case gpucfd, the project's GPU solver, was extended to compute turbulence directly and solved the problem first. OpenFOAM then ran the same problem on the same grid with the same time step.

The problem

  • Fluid between two flat plates at rest, pushed along the plates by a constant force. The box repeats along the flow and across it.
  • Fast enough to be turbulent: friction Reynolds number \(180\).
  • No turbulence model: the grid and the time step must resolve the swirls. The answers are averages over time.
  • Asked for: the mean speed, the speed at the centre, the friction coefficient, and the profiles of the mean velocity, of the swirl strength in the three directions and of the shear stress.
  • Reference: the published simulation of Vreman and Kuerten (2014), two independent codes.
  • Pass limits, set before the runs: 1 % for the two speeds, 2 % for friction and the peak, and a largest difference along each profile.

Results

On the same grid OpenFOAM meets 1 of 10 limits and gpucfd 5; gpucfd is \(7.2\times\) faster per time step. On a finer grid gpucfd meets all 10.

What it means
  • On the same cells and with the same time step gpucfd is closer to the reference: the mean speed differs by \(-0.34\,\%\) with gpucfd and by \(+4.48\,\%\) with OpenFOAM.
  • With both tools the swirls toward the wall and across the flow come out too weak on this grid, more so with OpenFOAM. With OpenFOAM the shear stress they carry is too low as well, so the flow is braked too little and runs too fast. The likely cause is that OpenFOAM's numerical scheme damps small swirls more than gpucfd's. That cause was not tested.
  • gpucfd reaches an answer inside every limit on this PC in \(1.7\) h. OpenFOAM's run on the coarser grid took \(5.2\) h, and the finer grid would take it about \(14.1\) h.
Limits
  • OpenFOAM uses the settings of its own channel tutorial, tuned only for speed (three timed tries of its pressure solver), not for accuracy. Another time scheme may do better: that would be a new test. It runs on 8 CPU cores, gpucfd on one desktop GPU: two different pieces of hardware in one PC.
  • OpenFOAM's mean speed was still changing during its averaging time: by \(+1.0\,\%\) from the first ten time units to the last ten. Its wall friction was within \(1.0\,\%\) of the driving force, inside the rule written before the run, so the run was not extended. A longer run would move its mean speed further in that direction.
  • The finer grid was run with gpucfd only. It is one grid, with no finer one to confirm it.
  • Averaging times differ between the runs (30 to 38 time units); the statistical error of the mean speed is about 0.2 %.
