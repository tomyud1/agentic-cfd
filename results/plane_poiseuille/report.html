1 Problem

Water flows between two very large parallel plates, driven by a constant pressure gradient. Find the steady flow and compare the simulation with the exact solution.

What we want to find

  1. aVelocity profile across the gap, \(u(y)\)
  2. bMaximum velocity
  3. cFlow rate per unit width
  4. dWall shear stress

Setup

  • Two stationary plates a distance \(H\) apart, so large that the flow depends only on the position \(y\) across the gap.
  • A constant pressure gradient \(dp/dx\) along the plates drives the flow.
  • Both plates are no-slip (the water sticks to them).

3 Theory

This problem has an exact solution. It is derived and solved symbolically by SymPy, not in the agent's reasoning, and checked automatically against the equation and both boundary conditions.

Governing equation: x-momentum for steady, fully developed flow
\[ 0 = -\frac{dp}{dx} + \mu \frac{d^2u}{dy^2}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad \mu \frac{d^{2}}{d y^{2}} u{\left(y \right)} = - G,\qquad G \equiv -\frac{dp}{dx} \]
The pressure force pushing the fluid forward is balanced by viscous friction between layers.
Boundary conditions: no slip at both plates
\[ u(0) = 0, \qquad u(H) = 0 \]
Fluid touching a stationary wall does not move.
Solution: integrate twice, apply both conditions
\[ u(y) = \frac{G}{2\mu}\, y\,(H - y) \]
A parabola: zero at the walls, fastest in the middle.
  • Solution satisfies the momentum equation \(\mu\, u'' = -G\)
  • Solution satisfies \(u(0) = 0\)
  • Solution satisfies \(u(H) = 0\)
  • Solution display form \(\frac{G}{2\mu}\, y\,(H - y)\) equals the derived solution

7 Comparison

Verification: the simulation is compared with the exact solution of the same equations.

How the differences are measured

Velocity-profile error
\[ \varepsilon_u = \frac{1}{u_{\max}}\sqrt{\frac{1}{N_y}\sum_{i=1}^{N_y}\big(u_i - u_{\mathrm{exact}}(y_i)\big)^2} \]
Typical (root-mean-square) difference over all cells, as a fraction of the peak velocity.
Flow-rate error
\[ \varepsilon_q = \frac{|q_{\mathrm{sim}} - q_{\mathrm{exact}}|}{q_{\mathrm{exact}}} \]
Relative error of the flow rate computed by OpenFOAM.
Observed order
\[ p = \frac{\ln(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{coarse}}/\varepsilon_{\mathrm{fine}})}{\ln 2} \]
\(\varepsilon \propto h^p\). \(p = 2\) means halving the cells cuts the error by 4.
Momentum balance
\[ \frac{|\tau_{w,\mathrm{lower}} + \tau_{w,\mathrm{upper}} - G H|}{G H} \]
Must be zero on any mesh: friction on both walls balances the driving force.

Where the error comes from. On every mesh the simulated profile is the exact parabola shifted up by a constant, \(\Delta u = \dfrac{G h^2}{8\mu}\) (cell-to-cell variation of the shift \(\le 1.3 \times 10^{-13}\ \mathrm{m/s}\), round-off). Inside the channel the method is exact for a parabola; the error enters at the walls, where the slope \(du/dy\) is estimated over the half cell between the wall and the first cell centre. Averaging the two middle cells cancels the shift, so the centreline velocity is exact on every mesh.

8 Workflow insights

Problems met while producing this report, logged at the moment they happened, with how they were solved and what future agents should do differently. These feed back into the workflow guidelines.

productionlogged 2026-11-10 (time not recorded)
Problem
All runs stopped at the 5000-iteration limit, "not converged"; wall shear stress 0.4 % off on the finest mesh although it must be exact.
Diagnosis
Two causes. (1) The stopping rule used normalised residuals of \(v\) and \(p\), which are zero in this flow, so their normalised residuals are pure round-off noise and never fall. (2) Under-relaxation (U 0.9) acts like a pseudo time step: for diffusion-dominated problems each iteration removes only \(\sim 1/N^2\) of the smooth error, about 0.3 % per iteration at 128 cells.
Fix
Judge convergence physically (flow rate stops changing, wall friction balances the driving force). Linear problem: remove relaxation; it converges in 1 iteration.
Lesson
Normalised residuals are meaningless for fields that are ~0. Under-relaxation cost grows like \(N^2\) on diffusion-dominated problems.
Guideline
Always define a physical convergence check (a conserved quantity or balance). For linear problems use relaxation 1.
productionlogged 2026-11-10 (time not recorded)
Problem
Removing relaxation crashed the solver with a floating-point exception on the first iteration.
Diagnosis
SIMPLEC (consistent yes) divides by \(A_P - \sum A_{nb}\), which is exactly zero for pure diffusion without relaxation.
Fix
Plain SIMPLE (consistent no) with relaxation factors 1.
Lesson
SIMPLEC needs either relaxation or a convection/time term.
Guideline
Do not combine SIMPLEC with relaxation factor 1.
productionlogged 2026-11-10 (time not recorded)
Problem
With 20 undamped iterations the 128-cell run drifted after converging (flow rate changing by \(10^{-9}\)).
Diagnosis
Round-off noise in the (zero) pressure field grows a little each undamped iteration and feeds back into \(\mathbf{u}\).
Fix
Stop after 5 iterations; the answer is exact after the first.
Lesson
More iterations is not always safer when nothing damps round-off.
Guideline
For linear problems run a handful of iterations and confirm with the physical check.
analysislogged 2026-11-10 (time not recorded)

Lesson
Never match cells by exact floating-point coordinates.
Guideline
Set writePrecision 12 and sample by index or with OpenFOAM sampling functions.
