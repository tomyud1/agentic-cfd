Agentic CFD progress report · 2026-11-15

Shock tube, air at rest released across a pressure jump (Sod's problem)

✓ Completed, with differences from what was expected

Goal

The first flow with shock waves. Step 1 of 7 on the way to a rotating detonation engine.

Solved first with OpenFOAM's shock solver, which the project had never used. gpucfd had no solver for a gas whose density changes. One was built the same day and run on the same problem and grids, with its settings chosen by a budget of twelve short runs fixed in advance.

The problem

  • A closed tube of air at rest, 1 m long. A thin wall in the middle separates high pressure on the left from low pressure on the right.
  • The wall is removed. A shock wave runs to the right and an expansion to the left. No friction and no heat conduction.
  • Asked for at half a millisecond: density, velocity and pressure along the tube, the states between the waves, where each wave is, whether mass and energy are kept, and how sharp the computed jumps are.
  • Reference: the exact solution of this problem.
  • Pass limits: written by the solver agent before its fine runs.

Results

  • OpenFOAM matches the exact solution to about a hundredth of a per cent, with every wave in the right place.
  • gpucfd, built for this case and run on the same grids, is two to three times closer still, and much faster.
  • One prediction of the agent was missed: OpenFOAM's pressure error shrinks more slowly with finer grids than it expected. The answer itself is accurate.

Four of the eight grids. Cells and time step are halved together, so each level costs four times the one before. A gpucfd step has three stages, an OpenFOAM step one. In one dimension the GPU is mostly idle, so gpucfd's times measure its overhead, not the card. gpucfd's times run from creating the solver to the final field; starting the GPU library adds 0.22 s once.

What it means
  • OpenFOAM's shock solver gets the exact answer to about a hundredth of a per cent, puts every wave in the right place, and keeps mass and energy exactly.
  • One expectation was not met: the pressure error fell more slowly with the grid than the agent had predicted before its fine runs. The answer itself is accurate.
  • gpucfd's new solver is two to three times more accurate on the finest grid, in half the time steps.
  • For the detonation engine: a shock is spread over 2 cells on every grid, and this solver needs half the time step its own examples use, or the pressure rings behind the shock.
Limits
  • The two tools were not given the same care: OpenFOAM ran as the solver agent set it up, on one core and not tuned for speed. gpucfd's method was chosen by twelve short tries.
  • Why OpenFOAM's error in the expansion falls slowly is not explained. gpucfd with the same kind of limiter does not show it, so it belongs to OpenFOAM's solver and not to the limiter.
  • One dimension and no friction. The time step is not converged: the full report says how much error is left.
