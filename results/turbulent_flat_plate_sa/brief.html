Turbulent boundary layer on a flat plate with the Spalart-Allmaras model (NASA TMR case 2DZP)

Verdict of the case: WARN

Goal

The first turbulent flow, computed with a turbulence model.

Solved with OpenFOAM on the grids NASA publishes for this test. gpucfd has no turbulence model, so it cannot run this case. For turbulence computed without a model, see the turbulent channel (case 17).

The problem

  • Air flows along a thin, smooth flat plate. The layer of slowed air on the plate is turbulent.
  • The swirls are not computed. A model (Spalart-Allmaras) stands for their effect, and the average flow is computed.
  • Asked for: the friction on the plate, its drag, and the velocity and model profiles in the layer.
  • Reference: NASA's Turbulence Modeling Resource, the same model in NASA's own codes.

Results

WARN. OpenFOAM's Spalart-Allmaras model, set to NASA's standard form, gives a grid-converged skin friction at \(x_1\) of \(0.0027286\), \(-0.028\,\%\) from NASA's incompressible reference (\(0.0027294\); combined grid uncertainty \(0.024\,\%\)).

What it means
  • OpenFOAM's version of the model gives NASA's answer to a few hundredths of a per cent.
  • The verdict is WARN, because that small difference is just outside the estimated grid error.
Limits
  • gpucfd has no column: it has no turbulence model, and for this thin-layer kind of flow it is predicted to be slower than OpenFOAM.
  • This checks the model's implementation against other codes. It does not check the model against a real boundary layer.
