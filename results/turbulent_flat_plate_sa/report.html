Key findings

  • Same model, same answer? Close, with a caveat. The skin friction at \(x_1\), taken to the limit of an infinitely fine grid, is \(0.0027286\), against \(0.0027294\) for NASA's incompressible FUN3D: \(-0.028\,\%\), with a combined grid uncertainty of \(0.024\,\%\).
  • What the finest-grid difference is made of. On the \(545 \times 385\) grid the two solutions differ by \(-0.023\,\%\): this solution's grid error is \(-0.007\,\%\), the reference's \(-0.012\,\%\), and \(-0.028\,\%\) remains. Compressibility is not part of it (the reference is incompressible); against NASA's Mach 0.2 results the difference is \(+0.84\,\%\) (CFL3D, finest grid), and NASA's own incompressible run differs from that result by \(+0.87\,\%\).
  • Grid convergence. \(C_f(x_1)\) changes by \(+0.016\,\%\) from G4 to G5, with observed order \(1.74\) and a grid-convergence index of \(0.008\,\%\). The drag coefficient changes by \(+0.011\,\%\) from G4 to G5; its observed order is \(0.95\), \(1.80\), \(2.19\) on the grid triplets G1 to G3, G2 to G4 and G3 to G5, so it is not yet regular (NASA notes the same for its codes: the skin friction is singular at the leading edge).
  • The model is the standard one. The solver's printed constants are NASA's and the \(f_{t2}\) term is on (OpenFOAM leaves it out by default). Near the wall the simulated profile follows the model's exact wall-layer solution to \(0.24\,\%\) (\(y^+ \le 100\)).
  • Every run passed its physical check. The drag from the wall shear stress equals the momentum lost by the stream to \(0.0020\,\%\) or better on all five grids.
  • One change at a time. Leaving out the \(f_{t2}\) term (OpenFOAM's default) changes \(C_f(x_1)\) by less than \(0.0001\,\%\); first-order advection of the turbulence variable, as in NASA's codes, moves the grid-converged value by \(+0.003\,\%\) (on G5 itself: \(-0.008\,\%\)); closing the upper side with a lid changes it by \(+0.26\,\%\).

1 Problem

Air flows at uniform speed along a thin, smooth flat plate. There is no pressure gradient. The boundary layer on the plate is turbulent, and the turbulence is not resolved but modelled: the time-averaged flow is computed (RANS) with the one-equation model of Spalart and Allmaras. Find the friction on the plate and the velocity profile in the boundary layer, and compare them with the results NASA publishes for this case, computed on the same family of grids.

What we want to find

  1. aSkin friction coefficient \(C_f\) at \(x_1 = 0.97008\) on each grid, and what it tends to as the grid is refined
  2. bDrag coefficient \(C_D\) of the plate on each grid
  3. cSkin friction coefficient \(C_f(x)\) along the plate on the finest grid
  4. dVelocity profiles at \(x_1\) and \(x_2\) on the finest grid, as \(u/U_\infty\) against \(y\) and in wall units (\(u^+\) against \(y^+\)), with the law of the wall
  5. eEddy viscosity relative to the viscosity, \(\nu_t/\nu\): its profile at \(x_1\) and its largest value along the plate
  6. fDoes the solver's implementation of the model give NASA's answer? State the difference that remains on the finest grid and what it is made of (grid error, compressibility, differences in the model or the boundary conditions)

Setup

  • This is a verification case: the question is whether the model, as implemented in the solver used, gives the same answer as NASA's reference codes. It is not a comparison with experiment.
  • Two-dimensional. Coordinates: \(x\) along the plate from its leading edge, \(y\) normal to it. Lengths are in units of the reference length \(L\).
  • Domain: the rectangle \(x_{\mathrm{in}} \le x \le x_{\mathrm{out}}\), \(0 \le y \le H\). The plate is the lower side from \(x = 0\) to \(x = x_{\mathrm{out}}\).
  • Lower side ahead of the plate, \(x_{\mathrm{in}} \le x < 0\): symmetry (no friction, no flow through it).
  • Inflow, \(x = x_{\mathrm{in}}\): uniform free stream of speed \(U_\infty\) along \(x\), with the model's turbulence variable \(\tilde\nu = 3\nu\).
  • Upper side, \(y = H\): the free stream (far field). The boundary layer stays below \(0.03\,L\), so this side is far away.
  • Outflow, \(x = x_{\mathrm{out}}\): the flow leaves at the pressure of the free stream.
  • Plate: smooth, no-slip wall. The boundary layer is turbulent from the leading edge on (no transition is prescribed) and is resolved down to the wall: no wall functions.
  • The Reynolds number is \(Re_L = U_\infty L / \nu = 5 \times 10^6\), so \(Re_x\) reaches \(10^7\) at the end of the plate.
  • Treat the flow as incompressible (constant density). NASA's main results are for a compressible flow at Mach 0.2; NASA also publishes incompressible results for one quantity. The reference file gives both and says which is which.
  • Turbulence model: Spalart-Allmaras in its standard form (NASA's name: SA). NASA states that the variant without the \(f_{t2}\) term (SA-noft2) gives essentially identical results for this case.
  • Grids: NASA provides a family of five nested grids for this case (reference/grids/, coarsest \(35 \times 25\) points, finest \(545 \times 385\)). Solve on these grids, so that the grid is not a difference between this solution and the reference.
  • Skin friction coefficient: \(C_f = \tau_w / (\tfrac12 \rho U_\infty^2)\) with the wall shear stress \(\tau_w\). Drag coefficient of the plate: \(C_D = F / (\tfrac12 \rho U_\infty^2 A)\) with the friction force \(F\) on the plate per unit depth and the reference area \(A = 2L\) per unit depth (the length of the plate).
  • Wall units: \(u^+ = u / u_\tau\), \(y^+ = y u_\tau / \nu\), with \(u_\tau = \sqrt{\tau_w / \rho}\).

3 Theory

The whole flow has no exact solution: the equations are nonlinear and coupled to the turbulence model. Theory still gives one exact result that tests the implementation directly. In the layer next to the wall the model has an exact solution, a straight line \(\tilde\nu = \kappa u_\tau y\), and with it the model's own law of the wall. SymPy checks it against the model's equation, and the simulation is compared with it in Section 7. The momentum balance over the domain gives the physical check of every run.

Governing equations: time-averaged (RANS) momentum with an eddy viscosity
\[ \nabla\cdot\mathbf{u} = 0,\qquad (\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{u} = -\frac{1}{\rho}\nabla p + \nabla\cdot\left[(\nu + \nu_t)\left(\nabla\mathbf{u} + \nabla\mathbf{u}^T\right)\right],\qquad \nu_t = \tilde\nu f_{v1} \]
The turbulent fluctuations are not computed. Their effect on the mean flow is modelled as extra mixing, an eddy viscosity \(\nu_t\) that varies in space and is far larger than \(\nu\) inside the boundary layer. The equations are nonlinear and coupled to the model: no exact solution of the whole flow.
The Spalart-Allmaras model: one transport equation for the variable \(\tilde\nu\) (NASA's standard form, SA)
\[ \mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla\tilde\nu = c_{b1}(1 - f_{t2})\tilde S\tilde\nu - \left[c_{w1}f_w - \frac{c_{b1}}{\kappa^2}f_{t2}\right]\left(\frac{\tilde\nu}{d}\right)^2 + \frac{1}{\sigma}\left[\nabla\cdot\left((\nu + \tilde\nu)\nabla\tilde\nu\right) + c_{b2}|\nabla\tilde\nu|^2\right] \]
Production by the mean shear, destruction near walls (\(d\) is the distance to the wall) and diffusion. \(\tilde S = \Omega + \tilde\nu f_{v2}/(\kappa^2 d^2)\) with the vorticity \(\Omega\); the functions \(f_{v1}, f_{v2}, f_w, f_{t2}\) and the constants are those of NASA's page (checked by code against the saved page).
Wall layer: the total shear stress is constant close to the wall
\[ (\nu + \nu_t)\frac{du}{dy} = \frac{\tau_w}{\rho} = u_\tau^2\quad\Longrightarrow\quad \frac{du^+}{dy^+} = \frac{1}{1 + \chi f_{v1}(\chi)},\qquad \chi = \frac{\tilde\nu}{\nu} \]
With no pressure gradient, the momentum carried by the mean flow is negligible near the wall, so the stress the wall feels is passed unchanged through the first few per cent of the boundary layer.
The model's exact solution in that layer, and its law of the wall
\[ \tilde\nu = \kappa u_\tau y\ \ (\chi = \kappa y^+),\qquad u^+(y^+) = \int_0^{y^+}\frac{dy'}{1 + \kappa y' f_{v1}(\kappa y')} \]
The model was built so that \(\tilde\nu\) is a straight line from the wall up; SymPy confirms it satisfies the transport equation exactly, with or without \(f_{t2}\). The integral gives \(u^+ = y^+\) at the wall and a log law above, with the intercept \(B_{\mathrm{SA}}\) listed below. This is what the simulated profile must follow for small \(y/\delta\), on any grid fine enough: a check of the implementation that needs no other code.
Drag from the momentum the stream loses (the physical check of every run)
\[ \frac{F}{\rho} = \int_0^{x_{\mathrm{out}}}\frac{\tau_w}{\rho}\,dx = -\oint_{\text{outer sides}}u\,(\mathbf{u}\cdot\mathbf{n})\,ds + \int_{\text{inflow}}\frac{p}{\rho}\,dy\quad\text{(+ small viscous terms)} \]
Integrating the momentum equation over the whole domain: the friction force on the plate equals the x-momentum that leaves through the outflow and the top minus what entered, corrected for the pressure on the inflow side (the outflow and the top are at the free-stream pressure, 0). The two sides are computed independently, the left from the wall shear stress and the right from the fluxes on the outer boundaries, so their agreement checks convergence and the force extraction together.
  • with \(\tilde\nu = \kappa u_\tau y\) and a constant total stress, the model's \(\tilde S\) equals \(u_\tau/(\kappa y)\) at every height
  • then \(r = \tilde\nu/(\tilde S\kappa^2 d^2) = 1\), so \(g = 1\) and \(f_w = 1\)
  • the model's transport equation is satisfied exactly (production \(-\) destruction \(+\) diffusion \(= 0\)), for any value of \(f_{t2}\)
  • at the wall the profile is \(u^+ = y^+\): \(du^+/dy^+ \to 1\) as \(y^+ \to 0\)
  • far from the wall it is logarithmic with slope \(1/\kappa\): \(y^+\,du^+/dy^+ \to 1/\kappa\)
  • the constants used here are the ones printed on NASA's page of the model
  • the inflow value \(\tilde\nu = 3\nu\) gives \(\nu_t = 3 f_{v1}(3)\,\nu\), the value NASA's page prints for it

Problem
On the coarsest grid the live stop reported every monitored value settled (changes of zero), yet the solver's residuals had stalled near one thousandth and the momentum balance was 8.5 per cent off.
Diagnosis
The iteration had locked into a cycle of period 2: the whole wall layer flipped between two states every iteration (wall-cell velocity changing by \(2\ \mathrm{m/s}\), the drag coefficient alternating between 0.00275 and 0.00318). The velocity equation and the turbulence equation are solved one after the other, and with the turbulence variable relaxed by 0.9 each over-corrects the other. The stop rule sampled every 20 iterations, always the same phase of the cycle, so it saw a constant; steady_check then reports an error of zero.
Fix
Relaxation of the turbulence variable 0.5 (velocity 0.9): residuals fall to \(10^{-12}\) and the momentum balance closes to \(1.7 \times 10^{-5}\). The stop rule now also needs the change between consecutive iterations to be below the tolerance, and samples at an odd interval (25).
Lesson
Monitored values that have stopped changing do not prove convergence when they are sampled at a fixed even interval. Residuals stuck at \(10^{-3}\) with 'zero' change were the sign; the momentum balance, the physical check, is what showed it plainly (8.5 % off).
Guideline
monitor.steady_check: also test the change between consecutive rows over the last samples and report 'oscillating' (or default to an odd interval). monitor.assess: warn when residuals are flat above \(10^{-6}\) over the last half of a steady run. Primer, steady runs: with a turbulence model the relaxation of the turbulence variable can cause a period-2 cycle on wall-resolved grids (0.9 and 0.7 cycled here, 0.5 and 0.3 converged); run the coarsest grid to its physical check before trusting a stop.
