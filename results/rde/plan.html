The next challenge: a rotating detonation engine

A plan: nothing on this page is a result yet

Goal

Simulate a rotating detonation engine with an AI agent: first a flat, two-dimensional version of it, with every result on the way checked against a known answer.

Prof. Frankel proposed this engine as a challenge: his lab studies it. Every flow the project has solved so far had constant density and no burning. This engine needs shock waves and burning together, which is new for the workflow, for our use of OpenFOAM and for gpucfd, our GPU solver. So we do not jump: we climb to it in seven steps.

What is this engine?

  • A jet or rocket engine burns its fuel in a steady flame. A detonation engine burns it in a detonation: a shock wave with a flame locked right behind it, moving faster than sound.
  • In a rotating detonation engine the chamber is a thin gap between two cylinders. Fuel and air enter at one end. One detonation wave runs round and round the ring and burns the fresh gas as fast as it arrives.
  • Why people want it: burning in a detonation is faster and promises a higher efficiency than burning in a flame.
  • The gap is thin, so the flow across it matters little. Cut the ring, unroll it, and the chamber becomes a flat rectangle. That is the two-dimensional problem.

The problem

  • A rectangle 12 cm long (once round the ring) and 4 cm high (along the engine). Its left and right sides are the same line.
  • Bottom: hydrogen and air, mixed in the exact ratio for complete burning, enter through small nozzles from a supply at 8 atm and 540 K.
  • Top: an open end. The burnt gas leaves, and no wave may bounce back in.
  • Start: a small hot spot at high pressure lights the wave. The run goes on until the wave repeats itself, turn after turn.
  • Asked for: the speed of the wave, the mass of gas passing through per second (in and out must balance), and the pressure and temperature along the ring.
  • Checked against: two published simulations of this kind of flat engine, and then a case from the lab whose answer the agent does not see.

The numbers are those of the 2D test in the first paper (its section 4.7) which used cells of 0.1 mm: 480 000 cells. The final problem statement is fixed with Prof. Frankel.

The steps: a ladder of seven problems

Each step adds one new difficulty, and each has an answer we can check. Each step is one case of the project's loop: a fresh AI agent solves it with the workflow and writes a checked report, then the tools and the workflow are improved with what it learned. Each step ends in a one-page report with accuracy and speed side by side.

Three ways to get a detonation solver

Step 4 needs a solver that handles a shock and burning together. We do not have one. We try all three ways below, and all three results go into the report.

AOpenFOAM's own burning solver
Installed already.

It was made for flames, not for shocks. The second paper shows that a solver of this kind can carry a detonation, on another branch of OpenFOAM than ours.

Risk: It may smear the shock or get its speed wrong.

BA published detonation solver
To be found, downloaded and built.

Research groups have built solvers for exactly this job on top of OpenFOAM. The first paper describes one.

Risk: That paper lists no public link to its code, so another one may be needed, and it may not build on our version.

COur own solver in gpucfd
To be written.

A GPU solver for detonations, open to everyone. The flat engine is a rectangle that repeats sideways: the kind of grid gpucfd is built for.

Risk: The most work. New code must pass steps 1 to 3 before it is trusted on step 4.

All three are run on step 4, the straight tube, on the same grid, because there the right answer is known without any simulation. Each gets a small tuning budget fixed in advance. The report shows accuracy and speed of the three side by side, whatever the outcome.

How we keep ourselves honest
  • Every step has a known answer, and its pass limits are written down before the runs.
  • Code computes every number in a report. Nothing is typed in by hand.
  • A step that fails is reported as failed. A verdict is never softened.
  • Tools are compared on the same problem and the same grid, each with a tuning budget fixed in advance.

Words used on this page

Shock wave
A very thin front where pressure, density and temperature jump. It moves faster than sound.
Detonation
A shock wave with a flame right behind it. The shock heats the gas so much that it burns at once, and the burning keeps the shock going.
Mixed in the exact ratio
Just enough air to burn all the hydrogen, with nothing left over.
Repeats
What leaves through one side comes back in through the opposite side. It stands for the closed ring.
Cell
One small box of the grid. The solver stores one value of each quantity per cell; smaller cells show finer detail and cost more.
Solver
The program that computes the flow, step by step in time.
Blind case
A problem whose answer exists but is hidden from the agent until it has reported.
Tuning budget
A fixed number of short timed tries to find a tool's best settings, so that the search has an end.
