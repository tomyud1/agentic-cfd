1 Problem

Water flows slowly through a small horizontal tube. After an unheated entrance section, the tube wall is heated electrically with the same heat flux everywhere. Find how the wall and the water warm up along the heated section and how the heat transfer coefficient (Nusselt number) develops, and compare the simulation with the exact solution of this problem.

What we want to find

  1. aLocal Nusselt number \(Nu(x)\) along the heated section, compared with the exact solution
  2. bNusselt number of the fully developed region, far from the start of heating
  3. cThermal entrance length: the distance from the start of heating at which \(Nu(x)\) comes within 5 % of its fully developed value
  4. dWall temperature \(T_w(x)\) and bulk temperature \(T_b(x)\) along the heated section, and both at the outlet

Setup

  • A straight horizontal tube of inner diameter \(D\). Coordinates: \(x\) along the axis, measured from the start of the heated section, \(r\) from the axis.
  • Inlet, \(x = -L_0\): water enters with a uniform velocity \(U\) over the cross-section and a uniform temperature \(T_{\mathrm{in}}\).
  • Unheated section, \(-L_0 < x < 0\): the wall is thermally insulated (no heat flux).
  • Heated section, \(0 < x < L_h\): a uniform heat flux \(q_w\) flows from the wall into the water, the same at every point of the wall.
  • Outlet, \(x = L_h\): the water leaves the tube freely.
  • The wall is no-slip (the water sticks to it). Gravity acts downward, perpendicular to the tube axis.
  • Local Nusselt number: \(Nu(x) = h(x) D / k\) with \(h(x) = q_w / (T_w(x) - T_b(x))\), where \(T_w\) is the wall temperature and \(T_b\) the bulk (flow-weighted mean) temperature at \(x\).

3 Theory

This problem has an exact solution: the extended Graetz problem for a uniform wall heat flux (Siegel, Sparrow and Hallman 1958), for a parabolic velocity profile, constant properties and no axial conduction. Its fully developed part is derived and checked by SymPy; the entrance region is an eigenfunction series (Kummer functions) evaluated with mpmath and checked against an independent finite-volume solution of the same equation. Nothing is taken from tables.

Energy equation (steady, constant properties, axial conduction neglected)
\[ u(r)\,\frac{\partial T}{\partial x} = \frac{\alpha}{r}\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\!\left(r\frac{\partial T}{\partial r}\right),\qquad u(r) = 2U\left(1 - \frac{r^2}{a^2}\right),\quad a = \frac{D}{2} \]
Heat carried along by the flow (left) balances heat conducted across the tube (right). The velocity is the fully developed parabola from the start of heating.
Boundary conditions
\[ k\left.\frac{\partial T}{\partial r}\right|_{r=a} = q_w,\qquad \left.\frac{\partial T}{\partial r}\right|_{r=0} = 0,\qquad T(0, r) = T_{\mathrm{in}} \]
Uniform flux into the water at the wall, symmetry on the axis, uniform temperature where heating starts.
Dimensionless form
\[ \eta = \frac{r}{a},\quad X = \frac{x\,\alpha}{U a^2} = \frac{4x}{D\,Pe},\quad \theta = \frac{(T - T_{\mathrm{in}})\,k}{q_w a}:\qquad 2(1-\eta^2)\,\frac{\partial\theta}{\partial X} = \frac{1}{\eta}\frac{\partial}{\partial\eta}\!\left(\eta\frac{\partial\theta}{\partial\eta}\right) \]
No parameter is left: one solution serves every tube, flow rate and heat flux.
Fully developed part (SymPy)
\[ \theta_{fd} = 2X + f(\eta),\qquad f(\eta) = - \frac{\eta^{4}}{4} + \eta^{2} - \frac{7}{24},\qquad \theta_b = 2X \]
The bulk temperature rises linearly (energy balance); the profile shape f does not change. The constant in f makes its flow-weighted mean zero.
Entrance region: eigenfunction series
\[ \theta = 2X + f(\eta) + \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} C_n R_n(\eta)\, e^{-\lambda_n^2 X/2},\qquad (\eta R_n')' + \lambda_n^2 \eta (1-\eta^2) R_n = 0,\quad R_n'(1) = 0 \]
The difference from the fully developed state decays along the tube. The eigenfunctions are Kummer functions; the first eigenvalues are 5.0675, 9.1576, 13.1972. 80 terms are summed; coefficients from \(\theta(0,\eta) = 0\) by orthogonality (weight \(\eta(1-\eta^2)\)).
Local Nusselt number
\[ Nu(x) = \frac{q_w D}{k\,(T_w - T_b)} = \frac{2}{\theta_w - \theta_b} = \frac{2}{\tfrac{11}{24} + \sum_n C_n R_n(1)\,e^{-\lambda_n^2 X/2}} \]
Infinite at the start of heating (the thermal boundary layer has zero thickness there), falling to \(48/11\) downstream.
  • \(\theta_{fd} = 2X + f(\eta)\) satisfies \(2(1-\eta^2)\,\partial\theta/\partial X = \frac{1}{\eta}\partial_\eta(\eta\,\partial_\eta\theta)\)
  • wall heat flux \(\partial\theta/\partial\eta = 1\) at \(\eta = 1\)
  • regular on the axis: \(\partial\theta/\partial\eta = 0\) at \(\eta = 0\)
  • bulk temperature \(\theta_b = 2X\): all heat put in since \(X = 0\) is carried by the flow
  • \(Nu_{fd} = 2/(\theta_w - \theta_b) = 48/11\)
  • eigenfunctions \(R_n = e^{-z/2} M(\tfrac12 - \tfrac{\lambda_n}{4}, 1, z)\), \(z = \lambda_n\eta^2\), satisfy \((\eta R')' + \lambda_n^2\eta(1-\eta^2)R = 0\) (relative residual \(< 10^{-12}\))
  • eigencondition \(R_n'(1) = 0\) holds for every \(\lambda_n\) (\(|R_n'(1)| < 10^{-15}\,\lambda_n\))
  • series converged: 60 and 80 terms give the same \(Nu\) to \(1 \times 10^{-16}\) at \(x/D \ge 0.5\)
  • an independent finite-volume solution of the same equation (400 cells, no series) agrees to \(4.2 \times 10^{-6}\) in \(Nu\) at \(x/D = 0.5\) to \(100\)

Why this choice
The exact solution has no buoyancy, and the main study verifies the simulation against it, so it must solve the same equations. Effect on the result
With \(g = 0\) the temperature is a passive scalar and the problem is axisymmetric. The 3D pair measures how much the real tube departs from the exact solution because of gravity.
